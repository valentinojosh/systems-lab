# Historical Projects

## Aurora University Remote-Study Community

During COVID-era remote instruction at Aurora University, I created and operated a Discord community that grew from a course-level study group into a 100+ member remote-learning space. The project ran for a full academic year, remained available into the summer, and supported academic collaboration when normal campus study networks were disrupted.

### Objective

Remote classes made it harder for students to find classmates, ask questions between meetings, and organize focused study sessions. The community provided a persistent place for Computer Science students—and classmates requesting calculus or other subjects—to coordinate academic work and maintain social connection.

### Growth

I began by inviting classmates directly during Zoom courses. The community reached approximately 20 members quickly, grew to around 50 within roughly one to two months, and continued expanding through member referrals and word of mouth. It later reached more than 100 members and sustained that scale for much of the academic year.

The growth was organic: members joined because the space was useful and invited peers who would benefit from it.

### Information architecture

The server evolved with member needs:

- subject-specific text channels, initially centered on Computer Science;
- additional subject areas such as calculus when requested;
- voice rooms for scheduled or ad hoc study sessions;
- general conversation channels;
- social and gaming spaces that helped preserve peer connection;
- user and administrator permissions appropriate to the community structure.

New channels were added when recurring demand justified them, keeping the environment navigable as membership expanded.

### Administration

I served as the sole administrator and moderator for the academic year. Responsibilities included:

- creating and organizing channel structure;
- configuring user/admin permissions;
- responding to member requests for new subject spaces;
- maintaining a usable balance between academic and social areas;
- moderating the community, with minimal incident burden.

Its value lies in requirements discovery, information organization, community administration, and sustained organic adoption.

### Results

The community transformed an invitation made during remote classes into a 100+ member collaboration space that remained useful throughout the academic year. It demonstrates technical community leadership: identifying a coordination gap, establishing a workable digital structure, adapting it to demand, and operating it consistently through a period of disruption.

### Professional relevance

- digital workspace and permissions administration;
- user-centered information architecture;
- organic community growth and onboarding;
- remote collaboration support;
- sole-administrator ownership;
- iterative service design based on observed demand.
